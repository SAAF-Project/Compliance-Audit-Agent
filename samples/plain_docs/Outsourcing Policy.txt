Outsourcing Policy

Document type: Policy
Owner: Chief Operating Officer
Version: 3.1
Approved: 2025-11-18
Classification: synthetic sample, not a real policy

1. Purpose

1.1 This Policy sets out the principles that Example Insurance Group (the
Company) applies when it entrusts an activity to a service provider instead of
performing it itself.

1.2 It aims to make sure that outsourcing does not reduce the quality of the
system of governance, does not unduly increase operational risk and does not
impair the ability of the supervisory authority to monitor the Company.

2. Scope

2.1 This Policy applies to all business units and legal entities of the Company
and to every outsourcing arrangement, including arrangements with other entities
of the same group.

2.2 Purchases of standard goods and services that the Company would not normally
perform itself, such as utilities or market data subscriptions, are outside the
scope of this Policy.

3. Governance and approval

3.1 This Policy is approved by the Board of Directors and is reviewed at least
annually.

3.2 The Chief Operating Officer owns this Policy and reports material deviations
to the Risk Committee within thirty days of their discovery.

3.3 Every outsourcing arrangement has a named business owner who remains
accountable for the outsourced activity throughout the life of the arrangement.

4. Critical or important functions

4.1 A function is critical or important when a defect or failure in its
performance would materially impair the continuous compliance of the Company
with its licence conditions, its financial performance or the continuity of its
services to policyholders.

4.2 The business owner documents the criticality assessment before the selection
of a service provider starts and has it confirmed by the Risk Management
Function.

4.3 The key functions of the system of governance may be outsourced only with
the prior approval of the Board of Directors.

5. Written agreement

5.1 Every outsourcing arrangement is laid down in a written agreement that is
signed before the service starts.

5.2 The agreement must grant the Company the right to audit the service
provider's data related to the outsourced function.

5.3 The agreement states the duties of both parties, the agreed service levels,
the notice period for termination and the rules for sub-outsourcing.

5.4 The service provider must inform the Company without delay of any
development that may materially affect its ability to carry out the outsourced
function.

6. Monitoring

6.1 The business owner monitors the performance of the service provider against
the agreed service levels at least quarterly and records the outcome in the
outsourcing register.

6.2 Table 2 – Register of critical outsourcing arrangements lists, for each
arrangement, the service provider, the business owner, the start date and the
date of the latest review.

6.3 Service level breaches that last longer than one reporting period are
escalated to the Chief Operating Officer.

7. Termination and exit

7.1 An exit plan may be prepared for critical outsourcing arrangements where the
business owner considers it necessary.

7.2 On termination, the service provider returns or destroys all Company data
and confirms this in writing.

8. Record keeping

8.1 The outsourcing register and all supporting assessments are retained for
seven years after the end of the arrangement.
